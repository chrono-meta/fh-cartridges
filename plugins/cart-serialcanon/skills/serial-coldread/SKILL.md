# A cold reader observes; the canon decides what the observation means

A reader who has seen the canon cannot tell you what a reader without it will think. So the order is fixed.

## 1. Give the reader only what a reader has

The reader gets the published episodes and the new episode — **no canon, no outline, no notes, no author
intentions**. Use a separate agent or a fresh session for it. Write down what it was given (its isolation level):
«episodes 1–N only» or, if anything else leaked in, what.

## 1b. Your own pass first — what model readers miss

Model readers rebuild meaning from context, so they miss two things a person trips on. Check them yourself:

- **Referents**: for each clause that drops its subject, object or referent, can you name who/what from this
  episode and the recap alone? If not, it is a defect, not restraint.
- **Time anchors**: a recalled scene needs an explicit time marker, or it blends into the present dialogue. Keep the
  source episode open while checking it.

## 2. Ask for observations, not fixes

Ask the reader to report, briefly:

- what they think is happening and who knows what;
- what confused them, with the line;
- what they expect to happen next, and what they think a set-up is for;
- where they stopped caring, if they did.

Do not ask «what should the author change». A reader's fix is usually wrong; their confusion is usually real.

## 3. Sort against the canon (now you may read it)

For each observation:

- **Design** — the canon says the reader should be confused or misled here (an open decision, a planted set-up).
  Not a defect.
- **Candidate** — the reader took something the canon does not intend (a wrong guess about a settled fact, a
  set-up read as paid off, a name they could not place).
- **Priority candidate** — two or more readers, or one reader plus the floor check, point at the same place.

With several readers, keep the **union** of candidates. Never vote: one reader's real confusion is not outvoted.

## 4. Report to the author

Candidates first, each with the line and the canon entry it touches. Design notes after. Taste («slow», «I liked
her») goes last and is labelled as one reader's taste. The author decides what to change.
